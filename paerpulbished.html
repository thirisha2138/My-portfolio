Research Paper 2025

Cognitive Socially Assistive Robot for Multimodal Assessment and Adaptive Support in Autism Therapy

Thirisha.R, Shaik Sameer Salhuddin, Dr. S. Janakiraman, Ms.N. Seenu, Dr.D. Devika, Dr. Rajesh Ramachandran

This paper describes a Cognitive Socially Assistive Mobile Robot (C-SAR) that focuses on improving therapeutic outcomes through multimodal assessment and interactive engagement for Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD). Built on a TurtleBot3 platform, it includes eye gaze tracking, facial expression recognition, and speech analysis to calculate Joint Attention Score (JAS), Social Engagement Score (SES) and Speech Clarity Score (SCS). During interventions, children engage in adaptive, game-like tasks using real-time audiovisual feedback. Validation demonstrates 78-85% speech imitation accuracy and 85-90% gaze fixation success rates, creating an engaging, objective platform to augment traditional therapy.

Research Paper 2025

AI-Based Multimodal Assessment and Assistive System for Autism Spectrum Disorder Students

Shaik Sameer Salhuddin, Thirisha.R, Ms.N. Seenu, Dr. S. Janaki Raman

This paper introduces an AI-based multimodal assessment and assistive system that can objectively quantify student progress in speech, body language, and social interaction for those with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD). The infrastructure combines depth cameras, microphone arrays, and wearable devices with advanced AI models for pose estimation, facial action unit detection, and speech prosody analysis. By delivering real-time adaptive assistance through visual prompts and audio cues—and providing comprehensive parent/therapist dashboards—the system aims to offer standardized, privacy-preserving, and scalable assessment to foster evidence-based interventions and inclusive education for neurodivergent learners.

Conference Paper 2024

Path Planning for Autonomous Drones

Thirisha.R, Shaik Sameer Salahuddin, Dr. S. Janaki Raman, Dr. N. Sivakamasudhari

This project compares two popular path-planning algorithms, A* and RRT (Rapidly-exploring Random Trees), for autonomous drone navigation. Simulations were used to evaluate their performance in dynamic 3D environments with obstacles, like those in search and rescue or inspection scenarios. A* systematically finds optimal paths using heuristics, excelling in structured spaces. In contrast, RRT rapidly explores high-dimensional spaces, prioritizing feasible paths over strict optimality. Key metrics revealed that A* is ideal for structured spaces, while RRT suits complex, high-dimensional scenarios.
